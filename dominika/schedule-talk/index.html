Part 1

Your softening toolbox

1.1 Read: six tools from our lesson

A soft startPrepares the person for bad news.
  • I’m afraid the schedule is already done.
  • Unfortunately, we need you on Saturday.
  • I see your point, but…
A request, not an orderThe same task, but the person feels respected.
  • Could you take the delivery on Thursday?
  • Would you mind coming in at 6?
  • I was wondering if you could close tonight.
Small softening wordsMake a hard message less heavy.
  • It might be a bit difficult this week.
  • The closing wasn’t quite finished.
  • I think we need one more person.
Instead of “no”A closed door makes people fight. Leave a small window open.
  • That might be difficult.
  • I’m not sure that will work.
  • Let me see what I can do.
“We”, not “you”The problem belongs to the team, not to one person.
  • We need two people for the night re-tagging.
  • Let’s check the tills together.
  • It looks like the tags weren’t changed.
Thanks first, then the requestPeople accept feedback better after a real thank-you.
  • Thank you for staying late. One small thing: …
  • I really appreciate your help, and next time could we…?

1.2 Make it softer: which answer is best?

Each phrase is too sharp. Choose the answer that is kind but firm. Be careful: too sweet is also wrong.

Part 2

Talking about the schedule

2.1 Read: phrases for schedule conversations

Listen and understand
  • I hear you. I know the early starts are hard.
  • Tell me more. Which day is the problem?
Explain why
  • The schedule depends on deliveries, re-tagging and footfall, so the hours change every week.
  • We need two people for every closing, so…
Remind them what you agreed, kindly
  • As we agreed when you joined, the hours are flexible and can change from week to week.
  • I know it’s not ideal, but the store has to be open, and that’s my responsibility.
Closed periods
  • I’m afraid we can’t approve holidays in December. It’s a closed period for the whole team, not just for you.
  • Let’s book your holiday for January now, so you get the dates you want.
Find a compromise and close the talk
  • I can’t change…, but what I can do is…
  • Let’s try it this week, and we’ll look at next week’s schedule together on Friday.
  • Thank you for telling me. Thank you for understanding.
